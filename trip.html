🍀 Practical Tips for Your Dublin Day
Currency: Ireland uses the Euro (€). Cards are widely accepted everywhere, but grab €30–50 from an airport ATM for pub cash and tips.
Luggage storage: The Excess Baggage Company is in Terminal 1 (not T2 where you land — take the 5-min covered walkway). Hours: Mon–Fri 9am–4:30pm, Sat–Sun 1–4:30pm. Alternatively, LuggageHero has drop-off locations near Trinity College (~€5/bag/day) that may be more convenient.
Red-eye survival: You'll be tired — pace yourself. The itinerary above is walkable and gentle. Dublin's compact city centre means nothing is more than 15 minutes on foot from anything else.
Weather: August in Dublin is mild (~18°C/64°F) but famously changeable. Pack a light layer and be ready for a brief rain shower. It will not ruin your day — it's part of the charm.
Taxi apps: Use Free Now (most common in Dublin) or Uber — both connect to licensed taxis only. Download Free Now before you land.

Tram 28 → Alfama & São Jorge Castle (exterior)
10:15 AM – 12:30 PM · ~2h
Hop on the iconic yellow Tram 28 from the Rua da Conceição stop in Baixa, about a 5-minute walk from your hotel — it winds through some of Lisbon's most photogenic streets all the way up to Alfama. Buy a Viva Viagem transit card rather than paying cash (cheaper). Continue up to Castelo de São Jorge and walk the exterior ramparts and gardens — you won't need a ticket to enjoy the approach, the walls, and the views from just outside the paid entrance; skip the ticket line and let the peacocks (often visible near the outer gate) be your photo op instead.

Belém: Jerónimos Monastery (exterior) + Pastéis de Belém
2:00 – 4:30 PM
Take a 15-minute Uber (or the tram/bus) west along the river to Belém. First stop: Jerónimos Monastery — a UNESCO World Heritage site and one of Portugal's architectural crown jewels. The facade alone is worth the trip: intricate Manueline stonework, maritime motifs, and the grand south portal are all fully visible (and free) from the plaza and gardens outside, no ticket needed since you're skipping the cloisters and interior. Then a 5-minute walk to Pastéis de Belém — the original bakery that invented the pastel de nata recipe in 1837. Eat yours warm with cinnamon and powdered sugar at the riverside. The Belém Tower is currently under renovation and closed to entry anyway — still worth a riverside walk past it for the photo.

🇵🇹 Lisbon Practical Tips
Getting around: Download the Bolt app (cheaper than Uber in Lisbon). For trams/metro, buy a Viva Viagem card (€0.50) and load it with credit — much cheaper than single tickets.
Tram 28 tip: It's famous and therefore crowded with pickpockets. Keep your bag in front of you. Consider doing the route early morning or taking an Uber for convenience.
August heat: Lisbon in August can hit 35°C / 95°F. Start early, carry water, wear sunscreen, and take a long mid-afternoon break at the hotel or in a café during the hottest hours (2–4pm).
Fado etiquette: Complete silence during performances. No talking, no phone calls. Applaud warmly at the end. It's an emotional and deeply cultural experience — treat it as such.
What to eat: Pastéis de nata (custard tarts), bacalhau (salt cod in dozens of preparations), grilled sardines, piri piri chicken, bifana pork sandwiches, Vinho Verde (light white wine), Ginjinha (cherry liqueur — sold in chocolate cups on Rua das Portas de Santo Antão).
Language: Say "obrigado" (men) / "obrigada" (women) for thank you. Never speak Spanish — Portuguese is its own proud language.

Getting a car

Bolt covers Porto, A Coruña, and Bilbao reliably. Uber, Cabify, and FreeNow also work in Spain. In small French towns (Pauillac, Saint-Jean-de-Luz) app coverage thins out — pre-arrange a taxi or ask venues to call one.

Bring Visa/Mastercard (Amex and Discover have patchy acceptance). Carry roughly €50–100 cash as backup, especially in Porto and small French towns where card readers are least reliable.
